Through a Sister's Eyes

A Testimony of Faith, Family, Love, and Never Giving Up Hope

Through a Sister's Eyes

Christine Gaydon
Co-Founder, No Judgement

"Addiction was the struggle. My brother was never the enemy."

Sometimes the hardest battles we face are not our own.

They are the battles we watch people we love fight, knowing that no matter how desperately we want to help, we cannot fight them for them.

For me, that battle was watching my brother struggle with addiction.

This is not simply a story about addiction.

It is a story about family.

It is a story about faith.

It is a story about loss, perseverance, prayer, and hope.

Most of all, it is a story about refusing to give up on someone you love.

Our family was built on faith, love, and the belief that family stands together through life's storms. We were not perfect, but we loved each other deeply.

Over the years I lost my mother, my father, and two sisters.

Each loss carried a heartbreak of its own.

When addiction began threatening my brother's life, I was not simply afraid of addiction.

I was terrified of losing another piece of my family.

"Twice, I came frighteningly close to losing him."

Living With Fear You Cannot Control

Anyone who has loved someone struggling with addiction understands a unique kind of fear.

The sleepless nights.

The unanswered phone calls.

The worry.

The fear that the next call will bring news you never wanted to hear.

There were times I cried.

There were times I prayed through tears.

There were times I questioned what the right thing to do was.

I wanted to help.

But eventually I learned something painful:

I could love my brother, but I could not make his choices for him.

The Person Behind the Addiction

This is something I wish more people understood.

My brother was never the enemy.

Addiction was.

He was still the brother I grew up with.

Still the son my parents loved.

Still a person with value, worth, and purpose.

I hated what addiction was doing to him.

I hated the pain it caused.

I hated the fear it created.

But I never stopped loving him.

And I never stopped believing his life mattered.

"People are more than the worst chapter of their story."

Faith Through The Storm

There were many moments when I wished my parents were still here.

I wanted their advice.

I wanted their comfort.

I wanted to hear their voices.

Even though they were no longer physically with us, I often felt as though their love continued to guide me.

When I prayed for my brother, I imagined how they would have prayed for him.

When I encouraged him, I thought about how they would have wanted him to know he was loved.

And through all of it, I leaned on God.

"Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand."

— Ephesians 6:13

Those final words became deeply personal to me.

"And having done all, to stand."

Sometimes standing was all I knew how to do.

When I could not change my brother's choices, I stood in prayer.

When I did not understand what was happening, I stood in faith.

When I was afraid, I held on to hope.

Why No Judgement Exists

Through these experiences I saw how quickly society judges people struggling with addiction.

People often see mistakes.

They see failures.

They see consequences.

But they do not always see the person.

They do not see the family praying at night.

They do not see the fear.

They do not see the hope.

And they do not always see the potential that still exists.

That is why No Judgement matters to me.

Not because accountability does not matter.

It does.

But accountability and compassion can exist together.

People deserve responsibility.

People also deserve dignity.

My Prayer

My prayer is for every family walking through addiction.

For every mother.

For every father.

For every sister.

For every brother.

For every person who feels exhausted from carrying hope.

I want them to know they are not alone.

And I want every person struggling with addiction to know this:

Your past does not have to define your future.

Today, as we build No Judgement, I pray that our family's pain becomes someone else's hope.

That our struggles become compassion.

That our experiences become understanding.

And that through God's grace, what once brought so much heartbreak may help bring healing to others.

Because every life has worth.

Every family deserves compassion.

And even in the darkest moments, there is still reason to hope.

Christine Gaydon
Co-Founder, No Judgement

Through Christ, all things are possible.
